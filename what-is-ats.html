What Is an ATS and How Does It Read Your Resume?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software that companies use to collect, sort and search job applications. Workday, Taleo, SuccessFactors, iCIMS, Greenhouse, Lever and Zoho Recruit are common examples. Almost every large employer, and many mid-size ones, use one.

When you click “Apply”, your resume usually goes into the ATS first. A recruiter then searches and filters inside that system. If the ATS cannot read your resume properly, or if it does not find the words the recruiter is searching for, your application may never be opened.

What the ATS does with your resume

  1. Parsing. It pulls the text out of your file and tries to split it into fields: name, phone, email, job titles, companies, dates, education and skills.
  2. Storing. These fields go into a candidate profile. Many systems show the recruiter this parsed profile, not your designed PDF.
  3. Searching and ranking. Recruiters type keywords such as “SCADA”, “IEC 104” or “Power BI”, or set filters such as years of experience. Some systems also score how well each profile matches the job description.

Why good resumes get lost

  • Text the parser cannot read: words inside images, icons, text boxes, or a scanned PDF.
  • Layouts that jumble the order: two or three columns, tables and side bars can make the parser mix lines from different sections.
  • Unusual section names: “My Journey” instead of “Experience” or “Toolbox” instead of “Skills”.
  • Missing keywords: you did the work, but you wrote “wrote PLC logic” while the job says “PLC programming”.
  • Contact details in the header or footer: some parsers skip these areas.

Does the ATS reject people automatically?

Usually a person makes the final decision, but the ATS decides what that person sees first. Some companies add “knock-out” questions (for example, notice period or a required certificate) that do filter automatically. In practice, a resume that parses cleanly and matches the job's words is far more likely to be seen.

How to make your resume ATS-friendly

  • Use a single-column layout with standard headings: Summary, Skills, Experience, Education, Certifications.
  • Save as a text-based PDF or DOCX (you should be able to select the text).
  • Put your name, phone, email and city in the body, at the top.
  • Use the exact skill names from the job description, where they are true for you.
  • Write dates in one clear format, such as “Aug 2024 – Present”.
