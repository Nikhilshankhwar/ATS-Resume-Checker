How to Tailor Your Resume to a Job Description (Step by Step)

Updated 19 November 2026 · Resume SAT

Sending the same resume to every job is the biggest reason for a low match. Tailoring does not mean rewriting everything. It usually takes 10–15 minutes per application once your base resume is strong.

Step 1: Collect the job's key words

Read the job post and highlight: the job title, required skills, tools and software, certifications, and repeated phrases. Words that appear two or three times matter most.

Step 2: Compare with your resume

For each highlighted word, check if your resume has it. Our checker does this for you: paste the job description and it lists matched and missing keywords.

Step 3: Use their words for things you really did

If the post says “client coordination” and you wrote “worked with customers”, change your wording to theirs. Same experience, words the recruiter will search for. Never add a skill you do not have.

Step 4: Update the summary and title

Put the exact job title near the top if it fits you, and mention 2–3 of their top skills in the summary. See summary examples.

Step 5: Reorder skills and bullets

Move the most relevant skills to the front of the Skills section, and the most relevant bullet to the top of each job.

Step 6: Check again and save a copy

Run the check again. Aim to cover most of the required skills, not every nice-to-have. Save the file with the company name, for example Rahul_Verma_SCADA_Engineer_LT.pdf, so you know which version you sent.

What not to do

  • Do not paste the job description into your resume in white text. Recruiters see the parsed text and it looks dishonest.
  • Do not claim years of experience or certificates you do not have; these are checked in interviews and background checks.
  • Do not stuff the same keyword ten times. Natural use in a few places is enough.
