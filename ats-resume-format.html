ATS-Friendly Resume Format for Freshers and Experienced (2026)

The best ATS resume format is simple: one column, standard headings, plain fonts and a text-based file. Design should help a human read faster, never hide information from the software.

Recommended section order

For freshers (0–2 years)

  1. Name and contact: phone, email, city, LinkedIn.
  2. Summary: 2–3 lines on your degree, core skills and the role you want.
  3. Skills: tools, software, languages and methods, grouped if the list is long.
  4. Education: degree, college, year, and score if it is good.
  5. Projects and internships: what you built or did, tools used, and the result.
  6. Certifications and achievements.

For experienced candidates

  1. Name and contact
  2. Summary (years of experience, domain, strongest skills)
  3. Skills
  4. Experience: newest job first, with 3–6 bullets each
  5. Projects (if they add something new)
  6. Education and certifications

Layout rules

  • One column. Two-column designs often get read in the wrong order.
  • No tables, text boxes or images for important text. Skill bars and star ratings carry no information for an ATS.
  • Contact details in the main body, not only in the page header.
  • Standard section names. “Experience”, “Work Experience” or “Professional Experience” are all safe.
  • Simple bullets (• or –). Fancy symbols may turn into question marks.

Fonts and size

  • Use common fonts: Calibri, Arial, Helvetica, Cambria, Georgia, Garamond.
  • Body text 10–11 pt, headings 12–14 pt, your name 18–24 pt.
  • Margins of about 1.5–2 cm. Do not shrink everything to fit one page.

Length

One page is ideal for freshers. Two pages are fine once you have several years of experience. Cut old or repeated bullets before you cut font size.

File type

A text-based PDF or DOCX both work with modern systems. Follow the job post if it asks for one. Never upload a photo or a scanned PDF: if you cannot select the text with your mouse, the ATS cannot read it either.

Personal details to leave out

In India many resumes still include date of birth, father's name, marital status, religion and a “Declaration” at the end. These add no value for most private-sector jobs and take space. A photo is also not needed unless the employer asks.
